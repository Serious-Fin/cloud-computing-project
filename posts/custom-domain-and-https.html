Custom domains, minus the headache

A free subdomain is fine for a mock. The moment you want people to remember the address, you want your own name on it. Here is what that actually involves.

Two records, usually

You add DNS records at whichever company you bought the domain from, and they tell the internet where to send traffic for your name.

  • Apex domain (example.com) — often an A record pointing at the host's address, or an ALIAS/ANAME record if your DNS provider offers one. The problem with a plain A record is that it hard-codes an address that can change.
  • Subdomain (www.example.com) — a CNAME pointing at a hostname such as your-site.netlify.app. CNAMEs follow the provider wherever their infrastructure moves.

If you are unsure, copy the exact values the dashboard shows you. Do not guess an IP address.

Then the certificate

HTTPS needs a certificate that matches your domain. Managed hosts watch your DNS records, notice the new name, and issue a certificate for it automatically through Let's Encrypt.

The certificate lives for about ninety days, and renewal is automated. The failure mode to watch for is silent: renewal breaks because a DNS record moved, and nobody notices until the browser shows a warning page.

Expired certificates are a solved problem, and they still take sites down every week. Automation only helps if you leave it alone.

Pick one hostname and redirect the other

Visitors will arrive at both example.com and www.example.com. Serving the same content on two addresses splits your analytics and confuses search engines, so redirect one to the other. Most hosts offer a one-line toggle for this. 

# On Netlify: Site configuration → Domain management
Add custom domain:  example.com
Alias:              www.example.com  →  redirects to example.com
HTTPS:              certificate provisioned automatically

Deployment previews out of the box

Once a repository is connected, every branch can get its own hostname. Open a pull request and you get a live URL for that exact commit — useful for showing a change to someone before it reaches the real site.

The short version

  1. Add the DNS records your host shows you.
  2. Wait for the certificate to be issued — usually minutes.
  3. Choose one canonical hostname and redirect the other.
  4. Add a yearly reminder to check that renewal is still working.

That last step is the only manual part left, and it takes about a minute.
